# Independent learning and conditional field operations

## Accepted direction

On 21 September 2026 the author asked for a hard critique of the actual course, then supplied a further critique distinguishing a narrow intellectual question from a repetitive surface theme. The author approved the revised plan with "perfect do it". The accepted question is:

> How can a team complete a fictional field operation when its information is incomplete, its equipment is unreliable, and its members have different responsibilities?

Observation, investigation, diagnosis and coordination connect varied lessons. Recovery is the final application. Repeating a collection and delivery loop does not by itself establish coherence, learning or an enjoyable agent experience.

## Problems the review identified

- Some student-facing rules disclosed the diagnosis that the assessment purported to elicit.
- Several "transfer" tasks changed an answer without asking the learner to reconsider a method.
- The council selected one prescribed agreement instead of comparing feasible terms and costs.
- Every final approach required all eleven checks, including transport work for a digital outcome.
- Short briefings and repeated timetables gave more detail to interface operation than to the subject.
- Completed scene travel and passing tests were being treated too readily as evidence of educational quality.

## Implementation decisions

The academic overview keeps the preferred in-person route and equivalent online academy. It now explains the central question, four connected capabilities and diminishing scaffolding. Academic information still precedes game entry.

The labs retain their distinct subjects and dated records. Observation adds a follow-up investigation; memory compares unaided recall, location cues and written records; mechanisms require a supported original diagnosis; circuit repair requires evidence before replacement and verification afterwards; negotiation permits several feasible agreements with different costs; route choice is judged against a declared task. Investigative labs can finish with their explained record. Apparatus and communication tasks retain consequential interventions where relevant.

The twelve briefings, lab plans and campus packs are aligned with these controls. Clear instructions name objectives, controls, evidence and completion conditions. They no longer need to provide a universal answer sequence. Worked solutions belong in labelled examples or facilitator explanations; underlying rules remain available.

A1 retains individual investigation; A2 adds an explicit receiver read-back dependent on the selected source. The final project selects capability requirements from the chosen approach and actual dependencies. Physical recovery requires a supported route; digital transmission requires power, authority and receipt; stabilisation requires support and an accountable continuing-care plan. Current source, custody, explained plans and outcome evidence remain necessary. Choosing a different ending cannot waive a dependency that still applies.

Demonstrations explain a method and the consequential difference in the assigned task. Added decisions compare a mnemonic with a written checklist, a ratio problem with blocked motion, competing council priorities, sensor verification with zero-contact routing, and relevant omissions in a changed final approach. Watch and Take Control remain separate records. For investigative examples, completion no longer waits for an unrelated package delivery; that extension remains available.

## Findings during implementation

| Actual observation | Change and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| The final metadata still required every preceding artefact even after the approach model became conditional. | Separate the four common prerequisites from the eleven available prior outputs; curriculum checks distinguish `requires` from `drawsOn`. |
| The fieldwork portfolio embedded earlier copies of revised demonstrations. | Resynchronise the authored portfolio examples and retain exact-content checks. |
| Changing a recorded diagnosis could preserve a cached success flag. | Invalidate that proof when the diagnosis changes and bind completion to the current supported diagnosis. |
| Measuring the repaired circuit again cleared its completed handover. | Treat measurements as observations; keep invalidation for actual equipment changes. |
| Restarting an old draft under the revised model would overwrite its original state. | Archive the old draft or record before replacement, include it in exports, and refuse replacement if the archive is invalid or full. |
| Several older browser assertions required deliveries, exposed uninspected settings, or expected a route for stationary stabilisation. | Update the assertions to verify the revised consequence, retained state and explicit optionality. Keep the underlying paused-action, fullscreen and no-script checks. |
| The compact relay described independent fault diagnosis although its interface supplies one combined measurement. | Require the Lab 5 diagnostic trace and accurately describe the compact assessment as measurement, replacement and verification. |
| A TypeScript string contained an unescaped apostrophe. | Correct the string and rerun the full type/build/course checks. |
