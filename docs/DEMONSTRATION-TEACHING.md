# Worked demonstrations: teaching contract

## What was authored

The catalog in `src/data/demonstrations.ts` contains **16 complete alternate examples and 116 teaching steps**: six steps for each of twelve labs, six for Fieldwork, ten for A1, ten for A2, and eighteen for the final project. Every example has a finished, downloadable Markdown artifact.

These are authored demonstrations, not records of real students, physical construction, assessed submissions, or observed playtests. Names, dialogue, errors, rehearsals, contributions, and outcomes inside each example belong to its fictional worked scenario. The actual assessment fixtures remain distinct.

Each demonstration supports two modes through the shared player:

- **Watch:** hear the student demonstrator explain the task before beginning, then follow their intention, precise action and observed result in each chapter. Narration starts with Watch or Play; mute and matching captions remain available. The conclusion connects the observations to the complete finished example.
- **Take over:** reproduce the reasoning by manipulating apparatus parameters, ordering actions, interpreting evidence, or confirming an exact factual detail.

Watching and independently completing controls are recorded separately. Neither is an academic grade. The completed artifact illustrates the expected quality and structure of a response; it is not a file to submit unchanged.

## Teaching pattern

Every step includes the current situation, the reason a decision matters, an answerable prompt, controls, causal feedback, a plausible mistake, a hint, and complete before/after scene states. Authored steps have an eighteen-second minimum, expanded by the player to allow time for the explanation and outcome. Playback pace, pause and replay remain available. Reading speed is not assessed.

The controls check the explicitly authored worked path. They do not pretend to judge the quality of an essay, a student's personal reflection, or every defensible alternative. Text checks are reserved for exact factual labels or codes. Open explanations remain visible teaching material and coursework evidence for human assessment.

Apparatus controls use the same names as scene values where practical, so changing a gear, interlock, angle, circuit power state, or level changes the represented apparatus. A wrong answer does not receive the successful authored ending. Numeric inputs have explicit bounds; options and ordering tasks expose the complete relevant item set.

Every example moves beyond revealing a correct answer. It demonstrates a consequential error or limitation, explains why it matters, and changes one condition so the learner must transfer the method.

## Operation Lamplight: cumulative reasoning

The final demonstration retrieves usable research from a storm-damaged star atlas. The six areas are Arrival desk, Workshop, Power bay, Record dome, Instrumented gallery, and Dispatch balcony.

Lena observes and navigates; Omar checks systems; Priya investigates identity and authority; Jonah coordinates agreements and handoffs. Their mission contract makes usable verified research, preservation, and custody the objective. Physical transfer is initially a preferred method, not an unconditional goal.

The eighteen steps incorporate every weekly capability:

2. Separate arrival observations from an unverified claim about the plates.
3. Repair the recall of equipment locations.
4. Orient and raise a connector.
5. Calculate and test a gear mechanism.
6. Diagnose, isolate, repair, and verify a nine-volt supply break.
7. Select an atlas version using its stated reference.
8. Repair permissions according to role.
9. Correct an asymmetric equipment handoff.
10. Reconcile disagreement with capacity and custody constraints.
11. Predict contacts along a declared instrumented route.
12. Preserve Version 1 and rehearse the physical transfer under its stated conditions.
13. Introduce the authoritative route and equipment disruption.
14. Diagnose the invalid dependency and preserve the original plan.
15. Respond to the amended identity requirement and agree on digital custody.
16. Create and rehearse Version 2.
17. Complete the revised recovery and confirm receipt.
18. Compare the final outcome with the objective, consequences, and remaining uncertainty.

The baseline rehearsal uses a verified eleven-unit cradle for a nine-unit original. The disrupted rehearsal withdraws that cradle and closes the east gallery; the remaining seven-unit carrier cannot support the original. A signed amendment also changes the required atlas reference from H3 to H8. The crew stops the outdated plan before movement, verifies the required digital copy, agrees custody, checks the west route, and records receipt. The original remains stable with its custodian.

The ending does not prove that every scientific claim in the atlas is true, that the equipment has been calibrated, or that a later physical handover is feasible. Those limits are part of the completed account. Both the baseline and disrupted rehearsal records remain visible; the original Version 1 is not silently overwritten.

## Scene fidelity contract

The scene frames are full physical snapshots. Within a room, stable apparatus properties and visible items are carried forward into later frames. A scene change does not accidentally erase a clock, paper, gear, or evidence ledger. The following conventions are shared with the renderer:

- `turns` is the number of driver input turns. Output turns equal input turns multiplied by driver teeth divided by follower teeth; an external meshing pair reverses direction.
- `interlock: true` means engaged. `cam` is degrees. `spring` states whether the required spring is attached.
- `voltage` is the declared source voltage. `fault` identifies the authored component fault; a dark open lamp does not imply zero voltage at its supply terminal.
- `ports` states a connector's initial directions. `orientation` is clockwise degrees; `level` is an independent height.
- Routes and sensors use string-encoded, zero-based cells on a five-by-five grid.
- Permission records identify role, action, and allow/deny status explicitly.
- `originalPlan` and `plan` are separate task arrays. The renderer can show the preserved and revised paths together.
- Memory items remain present in the frame even while `covered` hides them.
- Observation items contain their real clock time, note text, colour, and hatch state. A typed recall answer does not change what the original observation contained.
- Digital evidence props contain their declared version count, digest, and timestamp where relevant.
- Stable physical values may carry between steps; `verified`, `selected`, and other success claims do not automatically carry.
