## Before you arrive

Read E12 and assemble the final artefacts (30 minutes). Check each requirement on the capstone brief.

Bring mission contract, evidence ledger, seal model, interpreter model, crew agreement, plan v1, plan v2, observation audit, revised crew agreement, revised mission, revision memo. If you are entering this week directly, use the labelled example in the activity. It supplies a starting point and does not count as your original work.

## The two-hour lab

| Time | Work |
| --- | --- |
| 0–15 min | Read the case file and name the decision the evidence affects. |
| 15–35 min | Complete the final run, export it, and give the ten-minute team presentation. Each member answers a question about a decision outside their primary role before writing the individual defence. |
| 35–70 min | Complete the playable exercise below; record a reason before inspecting the feedback. |
| 70–95 min | Exchange explanations. Challenge one assumption using a specific source. |
| 95–110 min | Revise the output and explain which part of the feedback changed it. |
| 110–120 min | Save or export the record and write the closing reflection. |

## What leaves the room

**after-action** is this week's named artefact. Submit the final dossier, two runs, revision history, presentation and individual defence. Retain the archive as a record of how your judgement changed.

Include your decision, the case-file identifier, a justification, and one remaining limitation. The rehearsal export belongs beside your written reasoning; the ending does not earn the mark.

## How to judge the response

Retelling the operation chronologically without evaluating a choice makes the account difficult to assess. Claiming that success proves the plan was correct ignores alternatives and uncertainty.

A strong response explains a particular choice and an alternative. The supplied exemplar is inspectable feedback, not a sentence you must copy. Your confidence or reflection is never assigned an automatic academic grade.
