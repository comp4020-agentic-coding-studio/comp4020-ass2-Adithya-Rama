> The operation ends. The questions do not.

## What you will learn

- Defend a decisive choice using the evidence then available.
- Connect the course’s six outcomes in one coherent account.
- Separate personal contribution from the group’s outcome.

## The briefing

An after-action account reconstructs judgement from the information available at the time. Preserve the difference between a justified decision that met an adverse outcome and an unsupported decision rescued by luck. Both deserve analysis; neither can be understood from the ending alone.

The final defence connects the full semester: objective, evidence, model, crew, revisions and consequences. Select one decisive moment, name the available source, explain a rejected alternative and state a limitation. Your individual account should identify a contribution you can explain rather than summarise the group’s story.

## Worked example

A disclosure ending may satisfy a revised objective while leaving institutional control unresolved. The group can defend preserving claim continuity under E10 and still acknowledge the limit. An extraction ending must explain how its assumptions and crew agreement supported the decision.

## A plausible mistake

Retelling the operation chronologically without evaluating a choice makes the account difficult to assess. Claiming that success proves the plan was correct ignores alternatives and uncertainty.

## Before the lab

Read E12 and assemble the final artefacts (30 minutes). Check each requirement on the capstone brief. The full [case-file library](/resources/#E12) is available without sign-in.

Complete the final run, export it, and give the ten-minute team presentation. Each member answers a question about a decision outside their primary role before writing the individual defence.

## What happens next

Submit the final dossier, two runs, revision history, presentation and individual defence. Retain the archive as a record of how your judgement changed.
