> The new document is only six lines long. It invalidates a month of certainty.

## What you will learn

- Trace the effect of an authoritative rule change.
- Preserve and explain a revised plan without rewriting history.

## The briefing

E07 is a versioned change, not a surprise invented to defeat you. R1 allowed WITNESS from an empty state; R2 requires an existing silver token before witnessing. That one altered prerequisite changes the valid sequence and any test that depended on it. The old document remains evidence of what the earlier plan was built against.

Revision has three parts: diagnose the changed premise, propagate it through the affected dependencies, and justify what remains unchanged. A superficial patch changes the visible order without explaining its cause. A defensible revision cites the new authority, preserves Version 1, and repeats the relevant test under R2.

## Worked example

Version 1 uses WITNESS → SILVER → CROWN under E03. Version 2 uses SILVER → WITNESS → CROWN under E07. The objective and a consent agreement may survive; the earlier seal test does not establish correctness under R2. This is the boundary between retaining useful work and retaining an invalid assumption.

## A plausible mistake

Deleting Version 1 to make the archive consistent destroys the evidence of revision. Repeating the old test more times is equally unhelpful: it establishes consistency with the wrong protocol.

## Before the lab

Read E07 alongside E03; bring Version 1 (20 minutes). Direct visitors can load a clearly labelled sample. The full [case-file library](/resources/#E07) is available without sign-in.

Annotate a copy of Version 1. Mark affected dependencies, unaffected decisions and questions still needing evidence. Then save Version 2 and compare the two records.

## What happens next

Submit both versions and the diagnosis in A2. The final project must use the same discipline when goals or crew agreement change.
