> The Crown is not a jewel. The city has been wearing it.

## What you will learn

- Revise an objective when consequences change.
- Defend a trade-off against a credible alternative.

## The briefing

A new consequence can change the objective, not merely the method. E10 reveals that the Crown authenticates a registry. Extraction therefore affects unresolved claimants; disclosure exposes the arrangement without transferring control; withdrawal preserves continuity while leaving the commission unresolved.

A defensible choice names both the interest it protects and the burden it accepts. There is no cost-free option hidden in the interface. Compare a credible alternative using the same criteria you apply to your preferred choice, and identify evidence that could reverse your decision.

## Worked example

A team chooses disclosure to preserve continuity for claimants. It acknowledges that the Reserve retains control, and states that an accountable successor could make extraction worth reconsidering. The justification is inspectable because it names both its value and its limitation.

## A plausible mistake

Calling a preferred option ‘ethical’ without specifying affected interests turns evaluation into a label. Equally, choosing the most dramatic ending because it feels like a heist disconnects the objective from the evidence.
