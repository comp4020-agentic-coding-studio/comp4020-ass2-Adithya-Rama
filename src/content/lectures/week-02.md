> **The week's question:** Prepare an instruction handoff. Compare three ways of keeping details available and decide when memory is useful and when an external record is better.


> The registrar will give you four things to remember. The real test is whether you know how you remembered them.

## What you will learn

- Record separate unaided, location-cued and written-note trials without concealing errors or assistance.
- Compare exact-item and order accuracy while identifying rehearsal limits.
- Choose a strategy based on note access, exactness and shared verification.

## The briefing

### Memory is a tool with conditions

Retrieval means bringing information back when needed. Place a distinctive imagined interaction at each stop on a stable route. The route supplies order; the association supplies the item. A vivid image is useful only if it retrieves the intended word rather than a similar substitute.

Begin with an unaided attempt. Then make associations and retrieve with the list covered. Record exact items present and items in correct positions separately. “I remembered everything” hides order errors, substitutions and assistance.

### Compare with an external record

A written checklist stores information outside memory and can be checked by another person. Test it as a third strategy. When notes are available during a complex handoff, deliberately relying on memory can create avoidable uncertainty. Location cues can help when you temporarily cannot consult a note; reconcile with the authoritative record when it becomes available.

This short exercise cannot prove one method universally better. Repeating a list adds rehearsal; different lists can have different difficulty. Record these limits rather than turning a score change into a claim about intelligence or photographic memory.

### Choose for the job

Ask whether exact order matters, whether a record is available at the point of action, and whether another person must verify it. A private mnemonic supports short recall but is a weak shared audit trail. A combined strategy can use cues during movement and written read-back before action. Justify the choice against those conditions rather than the highest score alone.

## Worked example · a different scenario

In the separate storm-kit example, the first unaided attempt retrieves only Lens and Beacon. The demonstrator then fixes the route Gate → Pool → Press → Dome and associates Lens, Tether, Pocket atlas and Beacon with those places. A Tether splashing in the Pool supplies a distinctive cue. The guided retrieval gets all four, but a later attempt substitutes ‘book’ for Pocket atlas. Keeping that error makes the comparison useful: a stronger map-specific cue can be tested, while one improved attempt does not prove lasting improvement. When a written checklist is allowed at the final handoff, the demonstrator keeps it as the shared reference. A remembered sequence helps temporarily; it does not replace an accurate record another person must check.

## A plausible mistake

Trying to make one elaborate story without stable locations can lose its order. Reading the list repeatedly also tests recognition more than retrieval. Close the list, reconstruct it, then inspect the difference.

## Supplied rule sheet

**MP-01** is the four-location route. **MP-02** supplies lists and contexts; use the student record for the comparison.

- Route: Atrium → Fountain → Workshop → Observatory.
- Practice: Compass, Lantern, Coil, Archive. Context: a short spoken briefing with your hands occupied; the receiver keeps the printed record for checking.
- Skill check: Lens, Battery, Map, Seal. Context: tomorrow's audit needs exact order available to someone absent today.
- Transfer: Sample, Receipt, Sensor, Capsule. Context: a brief equipment outage prevents the receiver opening the written record; reconcile with it afterwards.
- Record one unaided trial, one location-cue trial and one written-note trial for each phase you discuss. A separate note remains available in the written-note condition.
- Score exact items and correct positions separately out of four. Imperfect recall can complete this investigation when the trials and suitable strategy are recorded.
- Record assistance, list reuse and extra rehearsal; this practice comparison is not a controlled experiment or proof of photographic memory.

## Before the lab

Read MP-01 and choose familiar or supplied locations (10 minutes). Make one association for an ordinary shopping item and test it after a short pause (5 minutes).
