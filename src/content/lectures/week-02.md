> Two witnesses describe the same demonstration. Both sound certain. Their accounts cannot both establish the cause.

## What you will learn

- Distinguish an observation, a claim and an assumption.
- Explain confidence using provenance and a possible disconfirming source.

## The briefing

Evidence is not a synonym for a sentence you believe. Begin with the exact statement. ‘The receipt records a witness token’ is an observation about a document. ‘Silver opened the cabinet’ is a causal claim. ‘The protocol will not change’ is a future assumption that may quietly hold the plan together.

Classify provenance before assigning confidence. High confidence cannot transform a claim into an observation. A second witness is useful only if their account adds independent information; two people repeating one story are not two independent confirmations. Your ledger should preserve the source, its limits, and the decision it affects.

## Worked example

E02 includes an observed receipt and an unseen interval. The receipt supports the presence of a recorded witness token before silver. It does not prove silver is sufficient, or that the record is complete. E03 gives a model to test that interpretation, but describes itself as provisional.

## A plausible mistake

Classifying all plausible statements as facts loses the distinction the assessment needs. Likewise, marking everything ‘uncertain’ avoids judgement. Identify what is directly recorded and then explain the assumptions required to use it.

## Before the lab

Read E02 and E03 (15 minutes). Underline statements about what happened and circle statements about why it happened. The full [case-file library](/resources/#E02) is available without sign-in.

Work independently before comparing classifications. When you disagree, quote the exact words being classified; do not argue about a broader claim neither of you was given.
