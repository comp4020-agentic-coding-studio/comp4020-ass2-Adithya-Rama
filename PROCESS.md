# Process overview

I chose the mastermind idea because I wanted a course with a strong identity that students would remember immediately. I was interested in skills people are curious about but rarely encounter together in a university course: observation, memory, mechanisms, electronics, digital investigation and coordinating a team. The fantasy was useful because it put those capabilities inside a situation where information is incomplete and a plan can fail.

My first approved direction concentrated on making and defending decisions under uncertainty. That became the original harness in [4c8495d](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Adithya-Rama/commit/4c8495d), followed by the dossier-based implementation in [bc31705](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Adithya-Rama/commit/bc31705). It provided a coherent operation, preserved revisions and accessible course pages. However, I was not satisfied with the result. I felt the course had lost the attraction of acquiring distinctive skills. A symbolic ordering exercise did not fulfil what I imagined when I asked for an engaging mastermind academy.

I therefore asked to redesign the curriculum before adding more presentation. I specified a broad combination of physical problem-solving, memory, spatial reasoning, investigation, communication, simulated surveillance and improvisation. I also asked for highly interactive three-dimensional labs, assignments and a final project. During planning I chose a third-person character, close-up equipment views and stylised cinematic environments. My learning loop became briefing, learning, practice, testing, application in a new situation and progress toward the final mission.

The replacement in [8aca1cd](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Adithya-Rama/commit/8aca1cd) turns that direction into twelve different activity models and a recovery mission. I retained the principle that a successful ending does not automatically demonstrate good work. Assessments now require practical evidence, diagnosis, transfer and explanation. Campus and browser routes target the same concepts, while the site avoids claiming that a simulation certifies physical dexterity. Shared role information supports collaboration in one browser, and a solo student can switch roles. That keeps the experience self-contained on GitHub Pages.

Several promises became concrete checks: every capability has a later application, model configurations change between practice and transfer, earlier plans survive revision, invalid imports leave existing work intact, and unfinished checkpoints are distinct from completed evidence. Keeping the simulation rules separate from the graphics allows the same activity to work through keyboard controls and the three-dimensional scene.

The agent's review also exposed mistakes that a passing build did not settle. Equal-sized display gears misrepresented a taught ratio; a phone slide table overflowed; and an open-lamp exercise initially confused supply voltage with a working load. The corrected circuit uses an isolated continuity check. These are documented agent observations in the [redesign decision record](docs/REDESIGN-DECISIONS.md), not playtesting I claim to have performed myself.

I later said the direction worked, but asked for more visual polish and practice that feels precise, personalised and enjoyable. I wanted every lab and assessment to include a complete alternate example, like the demonstration website provided alongside a website-building assignment. That became a studio where students can watch a guided three-dimensional example or take control, then inspect a finished submission. The examples use different conditions, and watching remains separate from completing the assigned work.

My judgement remains necessary for whether the academy is appealing and the skills feel worth learning. Automated tests establish particular behaviours, while rendered inspection checks readability and interaction. Neither proves originality, enjoyment or an HD. I want students to arrive for the unusual premise and leave more capable of understanding systems and responding intelligently when conditions change. Publishing remains my responsibility, so local verification is not presented as live acceptance.
